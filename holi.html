HOLI

Holi, widely celebrated as the "Festival of Colours," is one of India's most vibrant, joyous, and ancient festivals, symbolizing the arrival of spring, the triumph of good over evil, and the blossoming of love and forgiveness. Observed on the Purnima (full moon night) of the Hindu calendar month of Phalguna (usually falling in March), this boisterous two-day festival marks the end of winter and welcomes the abundant harvest season ahead. In the weeks leading up to the celebration, markets transform into a kaleidoscope of colors, stacked high with heaps of gulal (brightly colored powders), water pistols (pichkaris), and water balloons. On the eve of the festival, known as Holika Dahan or Chhoti Holi, communities gather around massive public bonfires at sunset to perform sacred rituals, sing folk songs, and roast green grains. This ritualistic bonfire symbolizes the burning away of all evil, negativity, and past grievances, paving the way for a fresh, joyous start the following morning.
