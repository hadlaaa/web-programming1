INDIAN FESTIVALS

.DIWALI

Diwali is the beautiful festival of lights celebrated by millions of people. The word Diwali means "a row of lights" in the ancient Sanskrit language. People celebrate it to show that good always wins over evil and light wins over darkness. During Diwali, families clean their houses and decorate them with colorful clay lamps called diyas. They also wear new clothes, burst crackers, pray together, and share delicious sweets with friends and neighbors.


.ONAM

Onam is a joyful ten-day harvest festival celebrated in Kerala, India. It marks the annual homecoming of the mythical King Mahabali, a kind ruler who brought peace and prosperity to the land. During this time, people decorate their homes with beautiful floral carpets called pookalam and wear traditional attire. A major highlight is the Onasadya, a grand vegetarian feast served on banana leaves. Complete with exciting snake boat races and traditional dances, Onam is a time of unity, gratitude, and happiness for families and friends.


.HOLI

Holi is a vibrant Hindu spring festival, widely celebrated across India and Nepal as the Festival of Colours, which signifies the triumph of good over evil, the arrival of spring, and the power of love.
