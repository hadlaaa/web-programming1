Vegitables

Vegetables are edible parts of plants—such as leaves, roots, stems, flowers, or seeds—that humans and animals eat as part of their daily diet. In the culinary world, they are defined by their savory flavor, which distinguishes them from sweet fruits, even though some "vegetables" like tomatoes, cucumbers, and bell peppers are botanically classified as fruits because they contain seeds. These plants grow in various environments globally and are consumed in numerous ways: raw in salads, boiled, steamed, roasted, fried, or preserved. Culturally and culinarily, vegetables form the foundation of most global cuisines, acting as primary ingredients, side dishes, or flavor bases like onions and garlic.A vegetable is any edible part of a plant consumed by humans or other animals as food, encompassing a vast array of vegetative structures including roots, stems, leaves, tubers, bulbs, and even immature flower buds. Unlike fruits, which develop exclusively from the fertilized ovaries of flowers and contain seeds, vegetables are defined primarily through culinary and nutritional classifications rather than a singular botanical blueprint. Because they represent entirely different anatomical organs of a plant, vegetables vary drastically in their cellular structures, nutrient profiles, and roles in plant survival, but they collectively serve as the primary source of dietary fiber, vitamins, and minerals in human nutrition.
