DIWALI

Diwali, also known as Deepavali, is one of the most significant and widely celebrated festivals in India, symbolizing the spiritual victory of light over darkness, good over evil, and knowledge over ignorance. Lasting over five consecutive days, this grand festival of lights brings families and communities together in a massive celebration of renewal. In the weeks leading up to the festival, people thoroughly clean, renovate, and decorate their homes and workplaces to welcome positive energy and auspiciousness. Thresholds are adorned with intricate, colorful rangoli designs made from colored powders, rice rice, or flower petals, while every corner of the house is illuminated with traditional clay oil lamps called diyas, modern fairy lights, and candles.The historical and mythological roots of Diwali vary beautifully across different regions, yet they all share the core theme of righteousness. In Northern India, Diwali marks the joyous return of Lord Rama, his wife Sita, and brother Lakshmana to their kingdom of Ayodhya after 14 years of exile and their epic victory over the demon king Ravana. To guide their path home on a dark, moonless night (Amavasya), the citizens of Ayodhya lit thousands of clay lamps, a tradition that continues today. In Southern India, the festival celebrates Lord Krishna's victory over the demon Narakasura, liberating thousands of captive maidens. For Jains, Diwali marks the day Lord Mahavira attained Nirvana, while Sikhs celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas, marking the release of Guru Hargobind Ji from imprisonment.
